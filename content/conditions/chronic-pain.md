## Overview

Stub for medical team review. Chronic pain disorders in children include amplified musculoskeletal pain, chronic widespread pain, some headache disorders, and pain that has outlasted the injury. The scope lists chronic pain disorders under Path 1 when there is an evidence base for behavioral treatment. Confirm the diagnosis is actually that, and not a first presentation of something else. Pain medicine, rheumatology, or the child's specialist should be in the story.

No training in the current map is specifically titled for chronic pain. Do not pretend the chronic-illness series covers it unless the team adds that link.

What the medical team still needs to add: which pain diagnoses you treat, your activity guidelines, and the red flags you want re-checked.

## Key treatments

Behavioral treatments with evidence include CBT for pain and, for some programs, graded activity and parent coaching. Physical therapy restores movement. Medicines are the prescriber's, and many families arrive hoping for a medicine that ends the pain. Sleep treatment matters. You do not prescribe, and you do not promise a pain score of zero.

## Treatment burden

Clinic shopping, missed school, a parent who has left work, and a child whose day is organized around the pain level. Repeated tests that found "nothing" feel like an accusation. Siblings resent the canceled plans.

## Adherence

Plans fail when the first step is too big, when the adults still respond to every pain behavior with total rest, or when the child hears that you think the pain is fake. Explain the goal as function with pain still possible.

## Mental health and development

Anxiety, depression, and medical trauma are common. They do not mean the pain is imaginary. Younger children show pain in behavior and clinginess. School-age children exit activities they used to be good at. Adolescents lose peers and identity. Developmental difference changes how pain is reported. A child who cannot describe an internal state still has one.

## Long-term effects

The risk is a life organized around avoidance: school exit, deconditioning, caregiver disability, and a pain identity that is hard to loosen. Some pain disorders remit. Do not promise that. Do not threaten lifelong disability either.

## Red flags

- New neurologic signs, bowel or bladder change, fever, unexplained weight loss, night pain that is new, or pain that is different from the known pattern. Back to the medical team. Do not call a new pattern "the pain disorder" by reflex.
- Suicidal ideation. Chronic pain raises that risk. Ask.
- A family giving medicines you did not know about, including relatives' prescriptions.
- Complete school withdrawal with no medical team in the loop.

## Therapy implications

### Individual

Validate the pain in the first session in a sentence the child can repeat. Then shift the goal to one valued activity. Teach a short coping skill they can use while moving, not only while resting. Watch for all-or-nothing effort.

### Family

Parent responses that remove all demands on pain days, and parent responses that accuse the child of faking, both worsen the picture. Coach a middle path the medical team agrees is safe. Caregiver fear of "missing something" needs a rule for when to call the doctor, so it is not every evening.

### Parent training

One activity goal. A calm response to pain reports. No quiz about whether it is real. Praise function. Keep the child's developmental level in the instruction: concrete for younger children, collaborative for adolescents.

## Intake questions

- Who has diagnosed the pain disorder, and what workup has already been done?
- What happens at home in the hour after the child says the pain is bad?
- What has the child stopped doing that they want back?
- Any new symptom that is not the usual pattern?

## Differentials

- Chronic pain disorder versus a new medical problem. New signs go back.
- Pain versus depression versus both.
- School refusal versus pain versus bullying versus a learning problem that pain is covering.
- For a child with diabetes, sickle cell, IBD, or cancer, do not file disease pain under this card without the specialist.

## Modifications

- No physical exposure past what PT or the physician allowed.
- Do not use the word fake, or a wink that means the same thing.
- Include the caregiver who is home on pain days.
- Say clearly when you are outside a recorded training and using supervision instead.

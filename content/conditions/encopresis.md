## Overview

Stub for medical team review. Encopresis is repeated passage of stool in a place other than the toilet, most often from chronic constipation and overflow. The child often cannot feel the leak and is then accused of doing it on purpose. A smaller group have soiling without constipation. GI or pediatrics should say which picture this is. Path 1 of the CSC scope includes encopresis because there is an evidence base for behavioral treatment beside medical bowel care.

The additional-topics training includes encopresis. The feeding and GI training is the second link because meals, fluids, and toilet fear travel with it.

What the medical team still needs to add: your cleanout protocol in therapist language, and when you want a therapist to send a child back for impaction.

## Key treatments

Medical treatment of constipation comes first: the cleanout and the maintenance laxative plan the prescriber wrote. Behavioral pieces include a toilet sit after meals, a non-punitive response to accidents, and treatment of toilet fear. You do not choose the laxative or the dose.

## Treatment burden

Laundry, smell, secrecy, and a parent who is furious and ashamed. School accidents end friendships. The regimen of medicine and sits has to happen every day, including days when everyone wants to forget the problem exists.

## Adherence

Families stop the laxative when the huge accidents pause, and the constipation returns. Punishment makes the child hide underwear. Ask what the last cleanout was and whether the maintenance dose is actually being given.

## Mental health and development

Shame is the dominant emotion. Some children look oppositional because they refuse the bathroom. Younger children need a caregiver to run the sit. School-age children can own a piece and still need an adult to keep the medicine going. Rule out developmental differences that make interoception or sequencing hard. Autism and intellectual disability change the teaching, not the need for a bowel plan.

## Long-term effects

Most retentive encopresis improves when constipation is actually treated and shame is reduced. Untreated constipation can go on for years. Soiling that starts a new psychiatric identity ("the kid who does this on purpose") outlasts the constipation if adults keep that story.

## Red flags

- Severe abdominal pain, vomiting, a swollen belly, or no stool at all. Possible impaction or obstruction. Medical care, not a sticker chart.
- Blood in stool, weight loss, or soiling that started with urinary symptoms. Back to pediatrics or GI.
- Suspected abuse. Soiling can be a signal. It is not proof. Use your reporting judgment.
- Suicidal ideation or bullying that has become dangerous.

## Therapy implications

### Individual

Externalize the constipation. The child is not the soiling. Teach what overflow means in concrete words, with a picture if that fits the developmental level. Work on toilet sitting and on the shame story. Do not require a confession.

### Family

The adult who is angriest needs a job that is not surveillance of underwear. Align all caregivers so one is not sneaking punishment. Explain overflow until the "on purpose" story loosens.

### Parent training

Scheduled sits, a calm response to a found accident, medicine as prescribed, and praise for the sit rather than only for a clean day. No public shaming. No withholding of meals as a consequence. Track accidents without a facial expression that punishes the report.

## Intake questions

- What did GI or pediatrics say: retentive constipation, or soiling without it?
- What cleanout and daily medicine were prescribed, and what is actually happening?
- What do adults do when they find soiled underwear?
- Any toilet fear, pain with stool, or a developmental difference that changes teaching?

## Differentials

- Overflow soiling versus deliberate smearing versus a developmental or sensory pattern. The response differs.
- Soiling versus diarrhea from laxatives that are dosed too high. That is a prescriber question.
- Encopresis versus IBD or another medical cause of loose stool. Blood, weight loss, and night stools go back to GI.

## Modifications

- Do not run a behavior plan in the absence of a bowel plan.
- Do not punish accidents.
- Match the explanation to developmental level. A long physiology lecture will not help a child who thinks in pictures.
- Use the encopresis material in the additional-topics training.

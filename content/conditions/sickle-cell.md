## Overview

Stub for medical team review. Sickle cell disease is a group of inherited red-cell disorders. Pain episodes, anemia, infection risk, and effects on the brain, lungs, and organs vary by genotype and by the child. In the United States it predominantly affects Black children, and many families have already been doubted when they reported pain. That history belongs in the formulation.

What the medical team still needs to add: the genotypes you see most, the home pain plan versus the emergency plan, hydroxyurea counseling points, and which school findings should trigger a hematology call about silent stroke.

## Key treatments

Common pieces include hydroxyurea or other disease-modifying medicines, penicillin prophylaxis in younger children, transcranial Doppler screening, pain plans, transfusions for some children, and, for a subset, stem-cell transplant or newer gene therapies. Doses and eligibility are hematology's. Fever is an emergency in many of these children because of infection risk. Use the family's written rule.

## Treatment burden

Pain can arrive without warning and cancel school, sleep, and plans. Clinic, labs, and imaging are frequent. Caregivers become experts and are still sometimes treated as drug-seeking when they advocate. Siblings and jobs take the strain. A child who looks well between crises is not a child without an illness.

## Adherence

Hydroxyurea and other daily medicines fail for ordinary reasons: taste, refills, doubt that a preventive medicine matters on a good week, and mistrust after being dismissed. Ask what happened the last time they sought care for pain. Do not open with a compliance lecture.

## Mental health and development

Depression, anxiety, and medical traumatic stress are common and under-asked. School problems may reflect silent cerebral infarcts, fatigue, missed days, or pain, not motivation. Learning and attention questions belong with neuropsychology and hematology, not only with a behavior plan. Adolescents face pain, opioids stigma, and transition.

## Long-term effects

Chronic organ effects, avascular necrosis, lung disease, and stroke risk continue into adulthood. Transition planning starts before the last pediatric visit. Cognitive effects of silent stroke can show up as the curriculum gets harder.

## Red flags

- Fever, trouble breathing, chest pain, severe headache, sudden weakness, trouble speaking, a prolonged erection, or pain that is not responding to the home plan. These are emergency patterns in sickle cell disease. Follow the family's hematology instructions.
- Suicidal ideation, or pain so uncontrolled the child cannot drink, walk, or sleep.
- Any request for you to change an opioid or hydroxyurea dose.

## Therapy implications

### Individual

Believe the pain report. Separate pain crises, fear of the next crisis, and low mood. Give the child language for school that does not require them to perform suffering. Do not use distraction as a way of implying the pain is optional.

### Family

Ask about prior experiences of biased care. Map the home pain plan so adults are consistent. Include siblings. Support the caregiver who is always the one to decide whether this night is the emergency department.

### Parent training

Coach a calm, rehearsed emergency script. Praise early use of the home plan rather than toughness. Keep one non-medical routine alive on good days. Do not punish school absence caused by a crisis.

## Intake questions

- What is the written plan for fever and for pain, and when do they go to the emergency department?
- Any history of stroke, abnormal transcranial Doppler, or a school concern about learning or attention?
- What happens when they say the pain is severe — at home, at school, and in the hospital?
- Which daily medicine is hard to take, and why?

## Differentials

- School refusal or "behavior" versus pain, fatigue, or cognitive change from silent stroke.
- Depression versus anemia, poor sleep from pain, or a recent crisis.
- Opioid concern versus undertreated pain. Hematology owns that line. Do not import an adult addiction script.

## Modifications

- Do not schedule a heavy exposure task on the back of a crisis week.
- Do not question the reality of a pain rating.
- Ask whether neuropsychology or a school plan is already in motion before you set cognitive goals.

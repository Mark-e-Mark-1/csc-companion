## Overview

Stub for medical team review. Traumatic brain injury ranges from a concussion that resolves to a severe injury with lasting cognitive and behavioral change. The mechanism, the severity, and how long ago it was all change the therapy. PM&R, neurology, and neuropsychology should be the source of what this brain can do right now. Families often need someone to say that irritability, fatigue, and a shorter fuse can be the injury.

What the medical team still needs to add: your return-to-school and return-to-play rules, and the late symptoms you want re-imaged or re-seen.

## Key treatments

Rest and a graded return after concussion, as the treating clinician wrote it. Rehabilitation therapies after more severe injury. Medicines for sleep, headache, tone, or mood only if a prescriber started them. Educational supports. You do not clear a child for sports or for a full school day.

## Treatment burden

A child who "looks fine" and is not fine. Repeated explanations to school. A caregiver who was also in the accident, or who feels guilty. Siblings living with a personality that changed. Appointments stacked on fatigue.

## Adherence

Return-to-activity plans fail when the child feels better for an hour and overdoes it, or when a coach overrules the restriction. Sleep recommendations fail when screens are the only thing that feels easy. Ask what the written restriction actually says.

## Mental health and development

Personality change, emotional lability, slowed processing, headaches, and sleep disruption are core, not side issues. Pre-injury ADHD, anxiety, or learning problems are still there and are now harder to see. Younger children may not have the words. Adolescents may be furious about restrictions and about friends who moved on. Substance use and driving become safety issues in teens.

## Long-term effects

Many concussions resolve. Repeated concussions and moderate to severe injuries carry lasting cognitive, behavioral, and headache risk. Neuropsychology over time is often more useful than a single early test. Adult outcomes depend on the injury and on how school and family responded. Do not predict from a story you heard.

## Red flags

- Worsening headache, repeated vomiting, seizure, weakness, unequal pupils, confusion that is worse than this child's baseline, or a child who is harder to wake. Emergency care.
- A new neurologic symptom months out that the team has not heard.
- Suicidal ideation. Ask. Impulsivity after injury can make this less predictable.
- Return to contact sports against medical advice. Tell the team. Do not negotiate the clearance yourself.

## Therapy implications

### Individual

Slow the session. One goal. Write it down. Fatigue is a stop signal, not resistance. Irritability gets a plan that assumes a shorter fuse, not a moral lesson. Rehearse the school script.

### Family

Teach the family that the behavior may be the injury, without taking away every expectation. Caregiver guilt and couple conflict are common. Protect siblings from becoming extra parents.

### Parent training

Shorter demands, a quiet recovery break that is not a punishment, and consistent limits on the one behavior that is dangerous. Do not ground a child for a post-injury meltdown in the same way you would have before. Do not drop all limits either. One or two rules, kept.

## Intake questions

- What was the injury, how long ago, and what restrictions are still in writing?
- What is different from before: sleep, temper, school, friends, headaches?
- Has neuropsychology been done?
- Who else was hurt, and who feels responsible?

## Differentials

- Irritability or inattention from the injury versus ADHD versus poor sleep versus depression versus a medicine effect.
- Headache flare versus a new injury or a neurologic complication. New or worse neurologic signs are not a coping failure.
- Avoidance of school versus cognitive fatigue the schedule has not caught up with.

## Modifications

- Short sessions. Written plans. Low sensory load.
- Do not do intense emotional exposure on a day the headache plan says to stop.
- Check the sports and school clearance before you assign activity.
- Use the developmental-complexity training to adapt pace. The injury may drop skills below chronological age. Keep the topics age-respectful.

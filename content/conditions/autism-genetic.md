## Overview

Stub for medical team review. Autism is a difference in social communication and in restricted or repetitive patterns, with a wide range of language, learning, and support needs. The scope counts it as a developmental difference that can meet Condition B. It is not, by itself, a reason to enroll a child in CSC if there is no mental health or behavioral disorder and no other qualifying path. High-hour ABA is an excluded service even when autism is present.

Genetic developmental disorders are not one condition. Fragile X, Down syndrome, 22q11 deletion, and others have their own medical surveillance. This stub is a placeholder for that whole shelf. Do not give syndrome-specific medical advice from this card.

What the medical team still needs to add: which syndromes your clinic wants as separate cards, and the medical red flags for each.

## Key treatments

Communication support, school services, OT, speech, and sometimes an ABA program delivered by an ABA provider. Co-occurring ADHD, anxiety, sleep problems, and irritability may be treated with therapy and, when a prescriber chooses, medicine. CSC's formats are individual, family, and parent training at an ordinary outpatient dose. Ask what is already in place so you do not duplicate or contradict it.

## Treatment burden

Appointments, school meetings, and a caregiver who is translating the child to every new adult. Sleep loss is common. Families may have been offered only one model of help and told that anything else is giving up. Siblings get less. The child is often in rooms where people talk as if they were not there.

## Adherence

Visual schedules and caregiver strategies fail when they are too complex to do on a Tuesday, or when every provider hands over a different one. Ask what is actually on the fridge. A plan the child cannot perform at their language level is not a refused plan.

## Mental health and development

Anxiety, ADHD, depression, and disruptive behavior are common co-travelers and are often the CSC referral. Sensory load, change, and communication breakdown drive "behavior." Intellectual disability may or may not be present. Giftedness may or may not be present. Measure the child in front of you. Use the Ahead / Typical / Behind lens rather than the word autism as a dose instruction.

Girls and adolescents are under-recognized. Masking at school and meltdown at home is a common pattern, not a contradiction.

## Long-term effects

Support needs change at puberty and at the transition out of school services. Some children have co-occurring epilepsy, GI disease, or sleep disorders that need their own medical home. Mental health risk, including depression in adolescents, should be asked about directly in language the person can use.

## Red flags

- New regression, a new seizure pattern, a stop in eating or drinking, or self-injury that is injuring tissue. Medical team.
- Elopement that has become dangerous. Safety plan with the caregiver before you leave the visit.
- Suicidal ideation. Autistic adolescents can be missed because the wording is concrete or unusual. Ask anyway.
- A request for CSC to provide many hours a week of ABA. Say no, and help them find the right referral. That exclusion is in the scope.

## Therapy implications

### Individual

Use the child's communication system. Short and concrete beats insight-shaped talk if language or processing says so. Anxiety treatment can be adapted. Do not require eye contact as a goal. Interests are a way in, not a distraction to extinguish for its own sake.

### Family

Parents may be in conflict about the diagnosis or about ABA. Hear it. The scope still excludes high-hour programs from CSC. Caregiver stress and the need for one consistent home strategy are the family work.

### Parent training

One antecedent change and one replacement skill, matched to communication the child already has. Visuals and a caregiver who can do the plan on a bad day. This is the behavior-modification training used inside a therapy hour, not the start of an ABA program.

## Intake questions

- How does the child communicate when calm, and when upset?
- What services and hours are already in place? Is this a request for ABA hours?
- What is the one situation that most needs to change?
- Any genetic diagnosis, seizures, GI disease, or sleep disorder the medical team follows?
- What are the child's interests and skills? Start there as well as with the problem.

## Differentials

- Aggression or self-injury versus pain, constipation, dental pain, seizures, or a communication system that fails under stress.
- ADHD versus sensory overload versus anxiety versus absence seizures.
- A learning disability alone does not meet Condition B. Autism does. Do not swap the labels to qualify a referral.
- Demand avoidance versus a demand that is too language-heavy.

## Modifications

- Predictable session structure. Show the schedule.
- Do not insist on eye contact, a still body, or spoken answers if another channel works.
- If an ABA team is involved, ask their goal so you do not contradict it, and do not absorb their hours.
- Use the developmental-complexity series first. Use the behavior-modification training for the functional view, with the exclusion in mind.

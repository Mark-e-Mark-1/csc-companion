## Overview

Stub for medical team review. Crohn's disease is a form of inflammatory bowel disease: chronic inflammation that can affect any part of the gut, with real tissue injury, bleeding, weight loss, and medicines that include immune therapies. Irritable bowel syndrome is a disorder of gut-brain interaction: pain and bowel-habit change without that inflammatory injury. They are treated differently. Do not let a chart say "stomach issues" and proceed. Ask which diagnosis GI has made. A child can be evaluated for both. You should not be the one who collapses them.

What the medical team still needs to add: your center's IBD medicine list in therapist language, infection precautions on immunosuppression, and how GI wants IBS pain explained.

## Key treatments

Crohn's: nutrition plans, steroids for flares, immune-modulating medicines, biologic infusions or injections, sometimes surgery. IBS: education, bowel-habit treatment, diet changes the dietitian owns, and behavioral pain treatments. Steroids and infusion schedules are IBD facts, not IBS facts. You do not adjust any of these.

## Treatment burden

IBD: infusions, labs, flare unpredictability, growth checks, and missed school. IBS: pain that is invisible, repeated testing, and a feeling of being told it is "just stress" after the workup. Both can dominate bathroom access and meals. Families of children with Crohn's may live from flare to flare. Families of children with IBS may feel dismissed. Different injuries.

## Adherence

IBD medicines slip because the child feels well between flares, because infusions take a day, or because side effects are miserable. An IBD flare from missed medicine is a GI emergency pathway, not a therapy topic to sit on. IBS plans slip when the diet is culturally impossible or when the child hears that the pain is imaginary.

## Mental health and development

Depression and anxiety are elevated in IBD, from the illness, steroids, and the life disruption. Pain, urgency, and accidents isolate school-age children. Adolescents care about body image, growth delay, and infusions. IBS pain is real pain. Stress can worsen it. Stress is not the moral cause, and saying so clumsily repeats the dismissal the family already got.

## Long-term effects

Crohn's can affect growth, puberty, bones, and, over years, surgical and cancer-surveillance needs that GI follows. Steroid courses have mood, sleep, and body effects. IBS does not injure the bowel in that way. Its long effect is disability from pain and avoidance if the child exits school and life. Do not mix these prognoses up.

## Red flags

- For known or suspected IBD: fever, blood in stool that is new or heavier, severe pain, vomiting, dizziness, or dehydration. Call GI or emergency care. Do not frame it as IBS anxiety.
- Unexplained weight loss, waking from sleep to stool, or blood are reasons to be sure IBD was actually evaluated. Ask GI. Do not reassure.
- Steroid mood crises: severe agitation or a dramatic personality change on prednisone. Tell GI.
- Suicidal ideation.
- Do not recommend a cleanse, an opioid, or stopping an immune medicine.

## Therapy implications

### Individual

For IBD, target flare fear, procedure fear, and the social problem of urgency, plus mood. For IBS, target pain-related avoidance and a return to activity, coordinated with GI so you are not overriding a real inflammatory picture. Bathroom planning at school is a concrete intervention for both.

### Family

Parents of children with Crohn's are often vigilant for a reason. Shape the vigilance into the flare plan GI wrote. Parents of children with IBS may need help stopping a total search for a missed disease once GI has closed that search, without calling the pain fake.

### Parent training

One response to pain that does not require the child to prove it and does not cancel every demand automatically. For IBD flares, follow the GI sick plan rather than a generic "push through." For IBS, graded return to school is often the behavioral piece, with a bathroom pass that is not a negotiation.

## Intake questions

- Which diagnosis is on the GI note: Crohn's, ulcerative colitis, IBS, or still being evaluated?
- What is the current medicine, including any steroid, and what is the flare plan?
- How does bathroom access work at school?
- What foods matter culturally, and what did the dietitian actually restrict?

## Differentials

- IBD flare versus IBS pain versus anxiety. The wrong one changes both the medical response and the therapy.
- Steroid mood effects versus depression, in a child on prednisone for IBD.
- Eating restriction versus fear of pain after eating versus ARFID versus a prescribed diet.
- School refusal versus urgency and accidents the child is hiding.

## Modifications

- Read the diagnosis before you teach a gut-brain explanation. That explanation helps IBS and insults a family in the middle of a Crohn's flare if it is your whole story.
- Do not design a diet.
- Ask about steroids this month before you interpret irritability.
- Use the feeding and GI training. Chronic-illness training is not the mapped set for this card unless the team adds it.
